The commercial model · the money

The money loop.

Money does not flow through NHSE. It flows directly between commissioner and supplier. A share of throughput returns to NHSE and pays for the framework. The model gets income only when the market sells.

Agreed in principle. Not final. The repatriation mechanics are the critical open item; see the flag below.
1
The commissioner pays the supplier
Payment is direct, for each activity, at or below the national maximum price. NHSE does not hold a budget and does not touch payments.
▸
2
The supplier pays the levy
The framework provider charges the supplier one to two percent of throughput. Suppliers are used to this type of charge. The charge applies only to sales.
▸
3
A part returns to NHSE
A platform fee: a percentage of throughput, taken from the provider levy. It is not a separate invoice to suppliers.
▸
4
NHSE reinvests it as contribution
It pays for the clinician panels, the validation service, the app integration and pathway change. It is possibly not sufficient for all NHSE costs. That is acceptable.
The money returns to the start and pays for the services that give the framework its value.
The critical assumption: repatriation of funds. These items are not decided: the name of the charge, the mechanism that returns the money, the VAT treatment, and the competition risk. The return line is theory until these items are decided. The model carries it as an assumption, not a certainty.
Why suppliers pay.
NHSE gives three things that other routes do not give: quality assurance and clinical validation; automatic permission for orders through the national app; and investment in pathway change that makes home sampling the standard route.
The language.
Any over-recovery is a contribution to development, for example the app. The return is a platform fee on throughput. It is never described in other terms.
The discipline.
The model must be simple to operate and transparent. If many people are necessary to calculate an invoice, the design is wrong.
